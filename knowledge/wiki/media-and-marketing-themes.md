# Gap Inc. Media and Marketing Themes

## The Dickson Marketing Philosophy

When Richard Dickson became CEO in July 2023, he brought with him the approach that had revived Barbie at Mattel: treat brands as cultural properties first, products second. His internal memo to Gap Inc. employees in May 2024 (announcing the Omnicom Media Group partnership) stated the company's ambition plainly: "fashion is entertainment, and they know how to work at the speed of culture."

The operative phrase across all Gap Inc. communications in FY2024 was "drive relevance and revenue" — connecting cultural moment-making directly to commercial outcomes. This replaced the prior era's posture of cost management and brand maintenance.

---

## Structural Marketing Changes (FY2024)

**New agency of record:** In May 2024, Gap Inc. retained Omnicom Media Group (OMG) as its cross-brand strategic media agency, replacing its prior fragmented agency relationships. OMG's client roster — Apple, Disney, L'Oreal, Chanel, PepsiCo, Vans — was cited as evidence of its culture-commerce expertise. The onboarding was completed by the end of Q2 FY2024.

**Marketing Shared Services organization:** Dickson simultaneously created a centralized Marketing Shared Services function reporting directly to him, covering shared media services, marketing intelligence, and marketing enablement across all four brands. The goal was to "leverage the scale of our platform while servicing individual brand needs" — using portfolio efficiency without diluting individual brand voices.

**Creative direction:** In February 2024, Gap Inc. hired Zac Posen as EVP and Creative Director, tasking him with revitalizing the visual identity across the brand portfolio. This was a notable hire, bringing high-fashion credibility to a company known for accessible basics.

## Brand-Specific Campaigns and Moments

**Gap brand — Linen Moves (Q1 FY2024):** Gap's Q1 FY2024 performance was driven significantly by the Linen Moves campaign, which asserted product leadership in linen ahead of spring/summer. CEO Dickson noted the campaign's impact directly in Q1 earnings commentary, crediting "strong marketing and product execution" as the primary driver.

**Gap brand — Troye Sivan loose denim (Q2 FY2024):** Gap's most culturally discussed campaign of the year enlisted Australian singer-dancer and Gen Z favorite Troye Sivan, alongside Dutch dance troupe CDK Company, to promote its loose-fit denim collection. The campaign appeared across digital channels and drove significant media coverage, including a Retail Dive feature story with Sivan's image as the lead visual. Dickson described Gap's denim campaign strategy as "repeating creative expressions that leverage our heritage, are rooted in music and dance, and declare a trend statement."

**Gap brand — Collaborations:** FY2024 saw Gap execute collaborations with Doen (a California women's wear brand appealing to loyal Gap consumers) and Madhappy (a Gen Z-facing wellness brand attracting new customers). Dickson said collaborations are "an important mechanism for the brand" and will continue as a strategic tool.

**Old Navy — Active and denim categories:** Old Navy's marketing leaned into its activewear and denim strength throughout FY2024, with Dickson citing "innovation and newness driving strength and market share gains" in these categories. Old Navy's active line has been a core component of its success for multiple years, and the brand entered FY2024 with momentum from Q1's 4.2% foot traffic increase, per Placer.ai.

**Banana Republic — Influencer marketing and store redesigns:** Following CEO Sandra Stangl's departure in early 2024, Banana Republic shifted strategy toward "getting back to the basics" under Dickson's oversight. The brand leaned into influencer marketing and redesigned select stores. The stated goal was "reestablishing the brand to thrive in the premium lifestyle space." Return-to-office mandates were seen as a tailwind for the brand's core office wear positioning.

**Athleta — Brand expression and activations (Q1 FY2024):** Athleta posted its strongest comparable sales quarter of the year in Q1 FY2024 (+5% comps), with earnings commentary citing customers responding "well to the new product, brand expression, and activations." The Q2 and subsequent quarters were softer due to lapping prior-year discounting, with full repositioning still underway.

## Search Interest Patterns and Retail Moments

Based on the brands' marketing cadence and product launches, search interest is expected to spike around the following retail events and campaign moments:

- **Spring (March–May):** Gap's Linen Moves campaign and Old Navy's active category pushes typically generate Q1 search interest spikes
- **Back-to-School (July–August):** Old Navy's core family audience and Gap's denim campaigns align with Back-to-School search peaks
- **Fall denim campaigns:** Troye Sivan's loose-denim campaign dropped in Q2 FY2024 (August timeframe), likely generating a notable search spike for both Gap brand and denim-related terms
- **Holiday (November–December):** All four brands compete heavily, with Old Navy and Gap historically driving the highest absolute search volumes; Q4 FY2024 showed Gap brand comps up 7%, its strongest quarter

## Sourcing Strategy as a Marketing Context

Gap Inc.'s supply chain evolution shapes what products it can market and how quickly. As of October 2024, Gap sourced apparel from 24 countries (up from 21 in 2021), with Vietnam (29% of sourcing value) and Indonesia (18%) as the two largest suppliers. China now accounts for less than 10% of products — a deliberate reduction that the CEO discussed publicly in early 2025.

Sourcing is category-specific: denim and woven bottoms come primarily from Vietnam and Bangladesh; performance wear and intimates from Sri Lanka (Athleta-adjacent); sweaters from China; woven apparel from India. This specialized structure supports Athleta's performance wear positioning and Gap's denim marketing narratives.

The shift toward Central America (Guatemala added 9 factories between 2021–2024) and a pledge to source $150M annually from Central America by 2025 reflects both nearshoring benefits (faster delivery) and tariff risk mitigation as a trade environment constraint. Tariff exposure remains a disclosed risk in FY2025 outlook.
